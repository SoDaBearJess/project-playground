05

Photo treatment

the shoebox years
finally organized!
1 Feature photos get the print treatment: a white frame, a soft shadow, and a slight tilt, like a snapshot laid on a table.
2 Captions are handwritten in Caveat, short and personal. They do the storytelling; the layout stays quiet.
3 Everyday images (cards, thumbnails) skip the frame and just use rounded corners, so the print style stays special.
4 Use real family-style photos: hands holding prints, albums, kitchen-table sorting. No sterile stock laptops.

07

Voice & tone

Deb's voice is the brand: first person, encouraging, and happy to help. The design should never sound more formal than she does.

Always

  • Write in first person: "I'm so glad you're here!"
  • Keep it simple — one idea per tip, plain words.
  • Encourage: progress over perfection, start anywhere.
  • Let a little humor through ("Ick!" is on brand).
  • End with an invitation to reach out.

Never

  • Tech jargon without a plain-English explanation.
  • Guilt about photo backlogs or disorganized libraries.
  • Corporate "we" — it's Deb, one person who loves this.
  • Walls of text; break everything into friendly steps.
  • Pushy sales language around newsletters or products.
